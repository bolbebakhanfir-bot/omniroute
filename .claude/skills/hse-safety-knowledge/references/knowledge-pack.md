# HSE Knowledge Pack: Water Park, Hotel and Theme Park Safety (Qatar context)
**For:** HSE Manager, Meryal Water Park (Rixos Premium Qetaifan Island North) and Azure Beach Club Doha
**Compiled:** 3 Oct 2026, from open, public sources

**How the sources were checked:** Many government sites (CDC, WHO, BLS, HSE UK, IAAPA, ILS) blocked direct downloads, so several figures come from search-result summaries. Before you quote a figure in a formal report, check it against the primary document linked in Section 12. "UNVERIFIED" means I could not confirm it.

## 2. Water slide and attraction standards

| Standard | Use it for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **EN 1069-1:2017+A1:2019** | Water slide design and testing | Main EU slide standard |
| **EN 1069-2:2017** | Slide operation, maintenance and instructions | Your slide operations manual should follow it |
| **ASTM F2376-24** | Water slide systems: classification through operation | Latest US edition |
| **ASTM F2461-23** | Aquatic play structures (slides ≤ 1.8 m) | Revision in progress |
| **ISO 17842-1:2023 / -2:2022 / -3:2022** | Dry amusement rides | **Does NOT cover water slides.** GSO adopted Part 3 as GSO ISO 17842-3:2023 (relevant for Qatar) |
| **EN 13814-1/2/3:2019 (+A1:2024)** | Dry rides: design / operation / inspection | |
| **ASTM F770-24** | Owner/operator duties: operation, maintenance, inspection, **operator training** | Use this for staff competence. *F1193 is a manufacturer standard.* |
| **UK HSG175 + ADIPS** | Inspection model | Design review → conformity → initial test → **annual in-service inspection (≤ 12 months)** → Declaration of Compliance |

**Slide operation essentials:**
- Daily pre-opening inspection **inside the flume**, with water off where the manufacturer allows.
- Weight and height rules, plus a rule on combined raft weight and weight distribution.
- Sensor-based dispatch with a traffic light rather than timer-only dispatch.
- Required staffing at the top and bottom of each slide. A slide does not run if a required post is empty.
- Wind, lightning and visibility shutdown limits from the manufacturer's manual.

## 3. Pools, spas and water quality

### CDC Model Aquatic Health Code (MAHC), 5th edition, Dec 2024 (benchmark until you have MoPH limits)
- **Free chlorine:** minimum 1.0 ppm (2.0 ppm if stabilised with CYA), maximum 10 ppm.
- **pH:** 7.2–7.8, target 7.4–7.6.
- **CYA ≥ 300 ppm:** remediate immediately.
- **Maximum turnover times:**
  - interactive water play: 0.5 h
  - wading and plunge pools: 1 h
  - activity pools and lazy rivers: 2 h
  - other pools: 6 h
  - diving pools: 8 h
- **Lifeguard zones:** drawn on a zone diagram. Each guard sees the whole zone and reaches its furthest point in **20 s**.
- **10/20 rule:** recognise a swimmer in trouble in 10 s, reach them in 20 s.
- **Diarrhoeal faecal incident:** close the pool and hyperchlorinate to **20 ppm for 12.75 h (CT 15,300)**.
- New in this edition:
  - portable slides banned
  - a clock visible to all spa users
  - artificial lagoons and single-pass splash pads now covered

### Spas and jacuzzis (highest risk: Legionella)
- CDC: **34% of treated-water outbreaks were linked to hotels and resorts, and 70% of those to hot tubs.** **All 13 deaths were from Legionella** (MMWR 2021, 2015–2019 data).
- Spa chemistry: free chlorine 3–10 ppm or bromine 4–8 ppm, pH 7.2–7.8, **tested at least twice a day**, with automatic dosing.
- Building water systems: **HSG274 Pt 2 / ACOP L8.** Hot water stored ≥ 60 °C and reaching ≥ 50 °C at the outlet within 1 minute; cold water < 20 °C. **HSG282** covers spa pools.
- **ASHRAE 188-2021 + Guideline 12-2023:** a written Water Management Program.

### Other standards
- **EN 15288-2:2018** (public pool operation): requires a hazard assessment and an *identifiable monitoring-record system*. This supports your KPI evidence.
- **WHO Guidelines Vol. 2** (2006): pools and similar environments.
- **UK HSG179** (4th ed., 2018): free download. Indicative lifeguard numbers: 25 × 12.5 m pool = 2; 50 × 20 m pool = 4 normal, 6 busy. Wave pools and free-form pools need their own assessment.

## 4. Lifeguard and beach standards (Azure Beach Club)
- **ILS** minimum competencies:
  - pool lifeguard (App. 08)
  - beach lifeguard (App. 10)
  - fitness testing (LPS 07)
- **RLSS UK NPLQ**; **Ellis & Associates** (the 10/20 rule, unannounced VAT audits).
- **Beach flags (ISO 20712-2 / ILS):**

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Red/yellow | Patrolled swim zone |
| Yellow | Medium hazard |
| Red | High hazard |
| **Double red** | Water closed |
| **Purple** | Marine pests |
| Red/white quartered | Evacuation / shark |

  **Green is not an ILS flag.** If you use it, explain it on beach signs.
- **Jellyfish in Qatar:** May–October. Species: *Chrysaora* cf. *caliparea* and *Catostylus perezi*. Hotspots include The Pearl, West Bay, Katara, Sealine and Al Wakrah. Take first-aid protocol from MoPH / Hamad Medical Corporation; vinegar use for these species is UNVERIFIED.

## 5. Heat stress (Gulf climate)
- **Legal:** stop all work at **WBGT > 32.1 °C** (all year), plus the summer midday ban.
- **ISO 7243:2017 WBGT reference limits for acclimatised workers.** These are **stricter than the legal limit for active roles**: rescues, roving, slide-tower climbing, F&B runners.

| Work level | WBGT limit (acclimatised) |
|---|---|
| Resting | 33 °C |
| Light | 30 °C |
| Moderate | 28 °C |
| Heavy | 26 °C |
| Very heavy | 25 °C |

- A Qatar study found **1 in 3 workers exceeded a core temperature of 38 °C** during a shift.
- Controls:
  - lifeguard rotation and shaded chairs
  - acclimatisation (≥ 1 week)
  - water and electrolytes
  - a WBGT log
  - buddy checks

## 6. Lessons from incidents

| Incident | What failed | Control at Meryal |
|---|---|---|
| **Verrückt, Kansas 2016** (child killed) | Slide rushed into service without engineering; raft went airborne; 13 earlier injuries ignored or suppressed; brake maintenance delayed | Management of Change and independent design review; raft weight-distribution rules; **trend minor injuries per attraction**; never suppress reports; close defects before reopening |
| **Bridal Falls, BC 2024** (cuts from a cracked flume) | Flume inspected from the side only; riders kept being dispatched after injuries; injury information not shared | **Close a slide after any laceration until inspected**; inspect inside the flume; first-aid reports go straight to ride operations and engineering |
| **Kenpark, Indonesia 2022** (slide collapse, 16 injured) | Worn structure; overloading | Corrosion and structural inspection of towers; control capacity on towers and slides |
| **Grand Rios, Minnesota** (drowning) | No attendant at the top of the slide | No slide runs without its required staffing |
| **Drayton Manor, UK 2017** | Child thrown from a rapids boat | Restraint and seating rules; enforce rider behaviour |

## 7. Benchmark data for KPI presentations
- **WHO:** about 300,000 drowning deaths a year worldwide (2021). Global rate down 38% since 2000.
- **CDC:** about 4,000+ fatal and about 8,000 non-fatal drownings a year in the USA.
- **IAAPA/NSC 2023 (dry rides):** **0.71 injuries per million rides**, 2.56 per million attendees; 8% serious; 29% of injuries happen while getting on or off.
- **NEISS water slide head and neck injuries (20 yrs):** 60% on public slides; lacerations 28%; mean age 13.
- **Water parks:** "about 1 in 250,000 rides" (IAAPA via law firms). UNVERIFIED, do not quote.
- **BLS 2023:** amusement parks (NAICS 7131) TRC **3.5 per 100 FTE**; hotels (7211) DART 2.3. US private industry 2024: 2.3.
- **UK HSE 2024/25:** accommodation and food has the **highest self-reported injury rate, 3,080 per 100,000 workers**.

## 8. KPI formulas

| KPI | Formula |
|---|---|
| LTIFR | LTIs × 1,000,000 ÷ hours worked |
| TRIR | Recordable cases × 200,000 ÷ hours worked |
| Guest first-aid rate | First-aid cases × 10,000 ÷ visitors |
| Rescue rate | Rescues × 10,000 ÷ visitors |
| Injuries per million rides | Injuries × 1,000,000 ÷ ride count (from dispatch counters) |
| Water-test compliance % | In-range tests ÷ tests due × 100 |
| Ride inspection completion % | Completed ÷ scheduled × 100 |
| Observation close-out % | Closed on time ÷ raised × 100 |
| VAT / audit pass rate % | Passed ÷ conducted × 100 |

There are no published industry benchmarks for the guest first-aid and rescue rates. Track your own monthly trend and year-on-year change.

**Leading indicators to report:**
- observations raised and closed
- near misses
- toolbox talks
- drills
- VAT audits
- heat-stress checks
- slide pre-opening checklists completed

Use the Bird ratio (1 : 10 : 30 : 600) only to show why near-miss reporting matters. Today the emphasis is on **SIF (serious injury and fatality) potential**.

## 10. Emergency preparedness
- **Lost child (Code Adam):** take a description, page "Code Adam", staff the exits, everyone else searches. **Call Police (999) if the child is not found in 10 minutes.**
- **Lightning:** clear all water and slides. Reopen **30 minutes after the last thunder**.
- **Shamal / dust:** peaks June–July; visibility can fall below 1 km. Follow Qatar Met Dept warnings and the manufacturers' wind limits.
- **AED:** shock within **3–5 minutes of collapse**. Survival falls 7–10% for every minute of delay. Place AEDs so the round trip takes ≤ 3 minutes; do not lock them away.
- **First aiders:** no official ratio (UK L74 says use a needs assessment). Base yours on attendance and response times.
- **Fire:** NFPA 101-2024 (hotel chapters 28/29) and NFPA 72-2025 (alarm and mass notification).
- **Brand:** Accor ALLSAFE (16 commitments, Bureau Veritas validated).

## 11. Corrections to common assumptions
1. Injury notification in Qatar is **immediate** (Police + Labour Department), not "24 h".
2. **ISO 17842 does not apply to water slides.** Use EN 1069 / ASTM F2376.
3. **ASTM F1193 is a manufacturer standard.** Use **F770** for operator training.
4. **Double red flag = water closed. There is no green flag** in the ILS system.
5. **ISO 7243 WBGT limits are stricter than the Qatar 32.1 °C cut-off** for physically active roles.
